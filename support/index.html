Support

Updated 30 August 2026

Need a hand? Email lift.fuel@outlook.com. One person reads it — Emmanuel Omar, who makes the app — and replies within about two business days. Tell us your iPhone model, the iOS version and what you were doing when it went wrong, and you will get a straight answer rather than a ticket number.

What Lift & Fuel is

An offline-first lifting and food logbook for iPhone. There is no account and no sign-in: your training, food, weigh-ins, recovery and profile live in the app's own database on your phone, and the food catalogue and exercise list are built into the app, so searching and logging work with no connection at all. Everything else is optional and stays off until you turn it on: Apple Health, barcode scanning, local reminders, and sync to a server you host yourself. No analytics, no advertising, no tracking of any kind.

Do I need an account or an internet connection?

Neither. No account, no sign-in, no password, and the app is fully usable on a plane. With sync off, the only time it touches the network is when you scan a barcode it does not already know — it sends that barcode number to Open Food Facts and nothing else. Server Sync is for people running their own Lift & Fuel server and is off until you set one up.

Where does my data live, and what if I lose my phone?

In the app's own database on the device. There is no cloud account behind it, so there is nothing for us to recover on your behalf — which is the trade for it being nobody's business but yours. What protects you is the automatic backup: once a day the app writes a dated snapshot of the whole logbook into its own folder and keeps the last seven. Those files are visible in the Files app and ride your iPhone's own iCloud or computer backup, so a restored phone brings them with it. For a copy you fully control, use Setup → Settings → Your data → Export everything.

How do I move my logbook to a new phone?

On the old phone, export everything — that writes a real .json file and opens the share sheet, so AirDrop, Save to Files or Mail all produce something that restores. On the new phone, put that file into the Lift & Fuel folder in the Files app, then open Setup → Settings → On-phone backups, where it appears under “Restore from a file”. Restoring is fill-only: it adds what the phone is missing and never overwrites anything already there, so the worst it can do is nothing. Strong and Hevy CSV exports can be dropped into the same folder and folded in the same way.

A barcode came back “not in the database”

A scan checks your own foods first — instant and offline — then asks Open Food Facts, a public food database. That message means nobody has catalogued the product yet; it is not a fault on your phone. Tap Enter it from the pack, type the numbers off the label once, and the barcode stays attached to that food, so the next scan finds it instantly and without a connection. “Couldn't reach the food database” is a different message and means a network problem — retry, or enter it by hand and it still keeps the barcode.

Why does the scanner want the camera?

Only to read the barcode while the scanner is open. Nothing is photographed, recorded or stored, and the camera is not used anywhere else in the app. If you declined and changed your mind, the scanner has a button straight to iOS Settings — or just search for the food by name.

What is Apple Health used for, and how do I turn it off?

It is optional, off until you connect it, and the scope is deliberately small. It reads two things: your body weight, which fills in any day you did not weigh in by hand, and how long you slept last night, which appears on Recover and feeds the readiness estimate. It writes back the weigh-ins you enter and the strength sessions you finish, so your other apps can see them. Anything read from Health stays on the phone and is never included in a server backup. To stop it: Setup → Settings → Apple Health → Disconnect. To revoke the permission itself, use iOS Settings → Health.

My reminders have stopped appearing

Almost always one of three things. Notification permission: the app asks once, and after a “no” iOS will not let it ask again — check iOS Settings → Notifications → Lift & Fuel. The iOS ceiling: iOS keeps only 64 pending notifications per app and silently drops the rest, so the app merges supplements that share a time and warns you in Alerts when you are near the limit. Or the reminder is simply skipped for today, which clears itself tomorrow. The quickest test is Alerts → Send a test in 5 seconds. You never need to re-tap “Schedule my reminders” after an edit; edits reschedule themselves.

The weight in one of my saved workouts changed by itself

That is deliberate. When a finished session sets a record on a lift, the saved workouts holding that lift learn the new weight, so next time you start where you actually got to. Three rules hold it back: it only ever goes up, so a deload or a bad day never lowers your plan; a workout you built with a different weight per set is left alone; and a record done for far fewer reps than the plan asks does not move it — a heavy single will not rewrite a 5×5. The finish screen lists exactly what changed and what was kept, and reopening that session puts the weights back.

The weight box suggests a number I did not type

That is the next-set suggestion, read from your last outing of that lift — advice for today, not a programme. If every working set hit the rep target it offers one real step up: 2.5 kg on a bar, Smith or cable, 1 kg on dumbbells under 10 and 2.5 above, 5 kg on a machine stack. Bodyweight work chases the next rep instead. If a set fell short it keeps the weight and asks for one more rep on your weakest set. Tap the line to load it, or type your own — nothing is applied until you do.

What does “Delete everything” actually delete?

Everything on the phone: training, food, weigh-ins, recovery, reminders, every setting, the sync token out of the phone's secure storage, and the app's own backup files — both the daily snapshots and the exports it wrote. It asks twice and cannot be undone in the app, so export first if you want a copy. Two things it cannot reach: backups already sent to a sync server, which you delete on the server itself, and a copy inside your device's own iCloud or computer backup, which stays Apple's until that backup rotates. Files you put into the app's folder yourself are left alone.

Something else, or a bug

Email lift.fuel@outlook.com with your iPhone model, your iOS version, and what you did just before it went wrong. Screenshots help. If it involves data you think went missing, do not delete anything first — say so in the message, because the dated backups on the phone can usually be read.
