2. Information the app handles

Depending on what you choose to enter, the app may handle:

  • profile details such as name, age, sex, height, weight, body-fat estimate and goals;
  • food, calorie, macro, water, supplement and reminder records;
  • exercise routines, training sets, cardio, recovery activities and readiness estimates;
  • weigh-ins, preferences and the version and time of your legal acceptance;
  • if you turn cycle tracking on, period start and end dates, optional daily flow, and any symptoms you log with an intensity from 1 to 5;
  • if you turn the step counter on, a daily step count and the steps and distance recorded while a cardio timer is running;
  • the coaching history: the weekly target adjustments the app proposed, what you accepted, and the calorie estimate it carried between check-ins;
  • and support messages and contact details you voluntarily send outside the app.

Some entries may amount to health information or other sensitive information under applicable law. Do not enter information about another person unless you are authorised to do so.

3. How information is collected

App information is collected directly from what you type, select or log.

Apple Health. If you grant permission, Lift & Fuel reads up to three things from Apple Health: your body weight, to help work out your daily energy use; how long you slept last night, which is shown on the Recover screen and used in its readiness estimate; and, only if you choose Apple Health as your period source in the Cycle card, your logged menstrual flow, so the app can place today in your cycle. The period read is a separate, additional permission that iOS asks for at the moment you choose it; declining it leaves every other feature working. Lift & Fuel writes back only the weigh-ins you enter and the strength workouts you save, so your other apps can see them; it never writes sleep or cycle information to Apple Health. Information read from Apple Health stays on your device: it is never included in the automatic backup file, never included in your export, and never sent anywhere by the app.

Motion & Fitness. If you turn the step counter on, the app asks iOS for Motion & Fitness permission and reads your step count, and the steps and distance recorded while a cardio timer is running, from the phone's own motion sensor. This is shown to you and used to estimate the distance of a walk or run. It is never sent anywhere and never changes your calorie target. The feature is off until you turn it on, and declining the permission leaves cardio exactly as it was: minutes, a typed distance and an estimate from time.

Face ID and your device passcode. If you turn on the optional lock for cycle data, the app asks iOS to confirm you are the device owner using Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. iOS performs that check; the app receives only a pass or fail result and never sees your face data, fingerprint or passcode.

Camera. The camera is used only while the barcode scanner is open, to read a product barcode. Nothing is photographed, recorded or stored. What happens to the number it reads, and the limits of what comes back, are set out in section 7.

Lift & Fuel does not access your contacts or precise location. It does not use advertising SDKs, analytics SDKs or cross-app tracking. Notification permission is requested only when you choose to schedule reminders.

4. How information is used

The app uses information to display your log, calculate general fitness and nutrition estimates, show trends and readiness, schedule local reminders, create your requested export, maintain app integrity. We do not sell personal information or use it for targeted advertising.

5. Storage on your phone

Your profile and logs are stored in the app's database on your device, and nowhere else. Protect your phone with a passcode and keep its operating system current.

The app's files are visible in the Files app. Lift & Fuel keeps its database and its automatic daily backups in its own folder on your phone, and that folder is open to the Files app under On My iPhone → Lift & Fuel. This is deliberate, so a backup is yours to copy, move or send somewhere safe without going through us. It also means anyone who can unlock your phone can read, copy or delete those files, including the cycle records described in section 6, without opening the app. Nothing is encrypted separately from the phone's own encryption. If that matters to you, a device passcode and Face ID or Touch ID are what protect it.

The Home Screen widget and the Live Activity. The rest timer can appear on your Lock Screen as a Live Activity, and a small widget can show the day's numbers on your Home Screen. iOS runs these as a separate extension, so the few figures they display — the rest countdown, and, for today only, the calories and protein still left against your target, the number of sets you have logged and your water total — are shared with that extension through a private storage area reserved for this app. It stays on your phone, it is reachable only by Lift & Fuel and its own extension, and it holds only what is on screen: no food log, no history, no cycle information, no profile. Tapping the widget's water button adds that water to today when you next open the app. Removing the widget and turning off Live Activities in iOS Settings stops both.

6. Cycle tracking

Cycle tracking is off until you turn it on, and is offered only where the profile sex is set to female. When it is on, the app holds the period start and end dates you log, any daily flow you record, and any symptoms you choose with an intensity from 1 to 5. This is sensitive health information. It is stored in the same on-device database as the rest of your log and is used to place today in your cycle, to adjust a recovery reading from the symptoms you logged, and, if you turn that option on separately, to add a small calorie allowance in one phase.

Cycle information is treated more restrictively than the rest of your log:

  • it never leaves the phone. It is not in the automatic daily backup file the app writes, and it is included only in an export you ask for yourself;
  • periods that came from Apple Health are never included even in that export, because they are Apple Health data and belong to the Health app; a flow day you logged yourself is your own record and travels with the rest of your log;
  • you may turn on an optional lock so the cycle section opens only after your phone's own owner check (Face ID, Touch ID or passcode). The lock hides explanations and history; a calorie or recovery adjustment already applied to today's numbers stays applied, because hiding it would make the figures on other screens wrong; and
  • “Delete cycle history” removes these records on their own, without touching the rest of your log, and returns the period source to manual so a later read cannot restore what you deleted.

The app presents cycle phase as context, never as a training instruction, and does not predict or infer pregnancy, fertility or any medical condition. Estimates are labelled as estimates and are withheld when the app's confidence in them is poor.

7. Barcode scanning and Open Food Facts

This is the only time information leaves your phone. Scanning is optional. You can log every food by hand and never scan anything.

When it happens. Only when you scan a barcode this app does not already hold. A product already in the app's own catalogue, or one you have scanned before, is resolved on the phone with no request at all.

What is sent. The barcode number, and a User-Agent line naming the app and the support address in section 14. Nothing else: no food log, no profile, no weight, no device identifier, no advertising identifier, no location and no account. There is no account to send.

Who receives it, and where. Open Food Facts, at openfoodfacts.org, a free and open food database run by the non-profit Open Food Facts association in France. This is a disclosure of information to an overseas recipient. Open Food Facts is an independent organisation, not our contractor and not under our control. It decides its own handling, logging and retention under its own privacy policy and terms, and under French and European law rather than Australian law. We do not control, and cannot undertake to you, how it handles a request once it arrives. By scanning a barcode you consent to that disclosure. If you would rather nothing left your phone, do not use the scanner.

What comes back, and its limits. Open Food Facts is contributed by members of the public. A product's name, brand, serving size and nutrition figures may be incomplete, out of date or simply wrong, and a product may be missing entirely. The information returned by a scan is not verified by us and is not a guarantee of what is in the product. The package label is authoritative. Anyone managing an allergy, intolerance, medical condition or anything else where an error would matter must read the current package label every time and must not rely on a scanned result. Any dietary tags shown in the app, such as gluten-free or dairy-free, are catalogue hints from the same sources and carry the same limits. A scan that finds nothing simply returns you to entering the food by hand.

A result is stored on your phone so the same product resolves offline next time. You can edit or delete it like any other food, and "Delete everything" in Settings removes it with the rest.

9. Retention and deletion

On-device records remain until you delete entries or uninstall the app. Cycle records can be deleted on their own with “Delete cycle history” in the Cycle card. Export records you want to keep first. “Delete everything” in Settings erases the app's database and the automatic on-phone backup files it keeps in the app's Documents folder; a copy inside your device's own iCloud or computer backup remains under Apple's control until that backup is replaced. Support correspondence is retained only as reasonably necessary for support, security, legal or record-keeping purposes: for up to 24 months after your most recent message, unless a longer period is required by law.
